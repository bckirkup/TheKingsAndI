# Onboarding — the medieval pack

**Second pack (D223 order). Voice spec — ships when its pack JSON lands.**
Voice: fealty, sworn service, the lord, the court, the field of honor.

## Who you are

You are the **lord** — the court's appointed hand over a company of sworn
pieces. Your career is three commissions; lose the hall's faith three times and
no lord will seat you again. The pieces call you "my lord" while they mean it.

## What the pieces call things

| In the world | You know it as |
|---|---|
| the company | your army |
| breaking oath | a piece quits the field |
| commanding against refusal | an override |
| hollow service | quiet quitting — lips swear, hearts don't |
| the tally | the campaign's closing ledger |
| the rout | the company scatters — commission over |
| release from service | dismissal — the suzerain takes command |
| unlooked-for mercy | grace — unearned, and never yours to claim |
| a sworn refusal | a free agent declines your banner |

## The voice

Courtly, oath-bound, grave. A medieval piece grieves in the language of broken
fealty — it doesn't complain about cost, it cites the oath you made it break.
Expect lines like:

> *"You swore to hold this file, my lord. I watched you spend it. I am forsworn no longer."*

> *"The move is lawful and so is my grief. I will not serve the one and not name the other."*

## What to listen for

- **The oath ledger.** Grievances are framed as what your orders made the piece
  forswear — loyalty framed as a contract you signed too.
- **Honor before arithmetic.** A medieval piece will hold a losing square
  longer than a military one will name it. Refusals sound like heresy trials.
- **The suzerain's shadow.** The King is further away here — dismissal reads as
  the lord being unseated, not relieved.
