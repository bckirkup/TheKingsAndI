# Onboarding — the military pack

**Default pack. Shipped first.** Voice: ranks, orders, the line, the field.

## Who you are

You are the **Commander** — appointed by the court, answerable to the King.
Your career is three appointments; dismissal at the last one ends it. The
pieces call you "Commander" to your face. What they call each other is your
problem.

## What the pieces call things

| In the world | You know it as |
|---|---|
| the roster | your army |
| desertion | a piece walks off the board mid-match |
| an override | forcing a refused order through |
| quiet quitting | a piece complies but stops trying |
| the Judgement Seat | the campaign's closing ledger |
| a rout | the army collapses — match over |
| dismissal | the King relieves you mid-career |
| grace | unearned relief — never yours to give or claim |
| a free agent declining | a recruit reads your record and says no |

## The voice

Terse, soldierly, hurt under discipline. A military piece does not monologue —
it reports its grievance like a casualty figure. Expect lines like:

> *"You forced my hand once, Commander. You will not force it twice — I leave."*

> *"I checked your arithmetic twice, Commander — once for the board, once for the heart. {san} fails both."*

## What to listen for

- **Rank solidarity.** Pawns talk about the line. If the line speaks, the file
  is listening.
- **The bill.** Casualty-grieved desertions cite who was spent before them.
  Military pieces keep ledgers.
- **Relief without credit.** Grace, when it lands, is unearned and explicitly
  not yours. The pack makes sure you know it.
