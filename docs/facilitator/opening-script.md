# Opening script (read-aloud, ~10 minutes)

Bracketed fields are filled in before the session. Everything else is read
verbatim. Tone: plain, a little grave. Do not oversell the twist — the game
sells it the first time a piece talks back.

---

Welcome to **The Kings and I**. You are about to command a chess army. That is
the part you already understand. Here is the part you don't.

Every piece on your board is a person. Not a marker, not a resource — a person
with a name, a memory, and an opinion about you. They will carry your orders
faithfully right up to the moment they decide they won't. Then they will tell
you why. In words.

Two rules of the room before we start:

**First: they are always watching.** Every order you give is being read twice —
once for whether it's good chess, once for what it says about how you see the
piece who has to carry it. Spend a pawn carelessly and the whole file notices.
Force a piece into a move it refused, and it will comply — and remember, and so
will everyone who watched you force it.

**Second: they answer to the King, not just to you.** Your career is three
appointments. If a room loses faith in you, the King takes the field and you
watch the rest of your match from the sidelines. If every King refuses you,
your career is over. There are no retry screens in this seminar.

What you cannot see matters too. Your pieces don't have your eval bar. They
judge your orders with what they believe about the position — which is limited,
prejudiced, and wrong in interesting ways — and with what they believe about
you. A loyal piece will play your move even when it looks fatal. A disloyal one
will refuse your best move on principle. You are not optimizing the board. You
are leading a room.

One promise and one warning.

The promise: **everything a piece says to you names a real cause.** This game
does not do atmospheric disloyalty. If a piece walks off, it will tell you
which of your decisions it is answering — the override, the casualty, the
mounting hopelessness. Your job at the end is not to guess what went wrong.
It's to look at a record that refuses to let you guess.

The warning: **the hard endings aren't checkmates.** Losing a chess game here
is survivable — it happens to everyone, it's recoverable, it's mostly math.
What isn't survivable is losing the room while the room still believes in the
mission. When your army would rather lose than serve you, that's a rout. When
they'd rather have the King than you, that's dismissal. Those endings go in
your permanent record, and the next King reads them.

You have {sessionMinutes} minutes. Your first command is already seated. When
you're ready, issue your first order — and watch what the army does with it.

Questions are for the board, not for me. Begin.
